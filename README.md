# CS255-System-Analysis

CS255 System Analysis and Design
Reflection
The DriverPass project focused on designing a system for a client who wanted to improve how students prepare for their driving tests. The client, Liam, the owner of DriverPass, needed a web-based system that allowed students to create accounts, purchase driving packages, schedule lessons, take online practice tests, and access training materials. The goal of the system was to provide students with structured preparation tools while also allowing administrators and employees to manage accounts, lessons, and system updates efficiently.
One aspect of this project that I did particularly well was translating business requirements into structured system documentation. In the Business Requirements Document, I clearly identified functional and nonfunctional requirements, constraints, and stakeholder needs. In the System Design Document, I effectively demonstrated how those requirements were transformed into UML diagrams and structured system components. I was especially strong in organizing system functionality and ensuring that each actor’s interaction with the system was clearly defined.
If I could revise one part of my work, I would refine my UML diagrams to make them even more detailed and visually polished. While they met the requirements, I would improve alignment, labeling clarity, and possibly include more relationships such as <<include>> and <<extend>> where appropriate. Strengthening these details would make the diagrams even more professional and easier for stakeholders to interpret.
When interpreting the user’s needs, I focused on understanding the client’s core problem: students were not passing their driving tests at a high rate. I translated that need into system features such as online practice exams, scheduling tools, and account management functionality. Considering user needs is essential in system design because the purpose of any system is to solve a real problem. If the designer does not fully understand the user’s goals, the system may function technically but fail to deliver real value.
My approach to designing software begins with gathering and analyzing requirements before jumping into design. I break down the problem into smaller components, identify stakeholders and actors, and determine how data flows through the system. I use techniques such as UML modeling, requirement analysis, and iterative review to refine the design. In the future, I plan to continue using structured analysis techniques while also incorporating more user-centered design strategies and feedback loops to ensure systems remain aligned with client needs.
This project strengthened my understanding of the relationship between business requirements and technical system design. It also reinforced the importance of clear documentation and communication when designing software solutions.
